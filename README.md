# Pine Hill — Sales Compensation Brief

A business case for aligning our high-ticket closers' pay with the value they create — moving from a flat salary to **base + per-unit commission**. Built for leadership. Every figure ties to our own build sheets or a cited industry source.

### 📊 Visual one-pager (share this): **https://enckequity.github.io/inside-sales-comp-benchmarks/**

> ### The idea, in one sentence
> Keep the **$75k base, cut nothing**, and add a **per-unit commission** that lands a solid closer around **$195k–$225k** (top closers $255k–$300k) — paid only on units actually delivered, with the company still keeping the large majority of every unit's gross.

> ### Why it's worth doing
> Each closer generates roughly **$10M in revenue and ~$2M in gross profit** a year. Today they earn a **$75k base + a 0.06% commission (~$80,760)** — about **4% of the gross they produce**, where comparable high-ticket dealers structure sales pay closer to **20–30%, with 25% the most common rate**. This brings our plan in line with the volume our team is now doing, in a way that's variable, self-funding, and still well within healthy selling-cost limits.

## How comparable dealers structure sales pay

The dealer standard is a **commission of 20–30% of front-end gross profit, 25% most common** — confirmed across **auto, RV, powersports, and farm/heavy equipment**. The single best parallel: *Farm Equipment* magazine quotes named equipment-dealer principals paying "25% of gross margin on new, 30% on used." (All citations: [sources.md](sources.md).)

| Reference point | Sales pay as % of gross |
|---|---|
| **Pine Hill today** (total pay ÷ gross) | ~4% |
| Powersports | ~15% |
| Auto / RV | 20–30% |
| Farm / heavy equipment | 25–30% |

Our plan predates this level of volume — this brief is simply about catching up to it.

## The recommended plan

- **Base: $75,000 — unchanged.** Nothing is cut.
- **+ $1,000–$1,250 per unit delivered** (≈ 5–6% of gross). Pure variable: **$0 cost on units we don't sell.**
- **It helps fund itself.** Factory options carry **36.95% margin vs. 26.55%** on base units; paying closers on what they deliver rewards attaching more high-margin options, which grows gross per deal.

## Questions leadership will reasonably ask (full answers [here](docs/questions.md))

| Question | The short answer |
|---|---|
| *Do we really know our margins well enough?* | Yes — they're on every build sheet (**26.55%** base, **36.95%** options). And the plan is a **flat per-unit** amount, so it doesn't depend on margin precision; it stays affordable at 18–27%. |
| *Isn't $75k already solid pay?* | It genuinely is. The narrower question is whether it reflects the **~$2M of gross** each closer generates, and what it takes to retain them — a competitor could offer $150k and still come out ahead. |
| *Leads are inbound and expensive — does that change it?* | A fair point, and it's built in: the plan pays a **closing rate (~5–6%), not a hunter's rate**. And keeping a proven closer **protects the return on our lead spend** — they convert more of those paid leads. |
