# Questions Leadership Will Reasonably Ask

These are fair questions, and each has a clear, data-backed answer. The goal here isn't to "win" — it's to give leadership everything they need to make a confident call.

---

## 1. "Do we really know our margins well enough to base pay on them?"

We do — they're on every build sheet that comes out of our pricing system:

| | Price | Cost | Gross | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base unit | $76,240 | $56,000 | $20,240 | **26.55%** |
| Factory options | $10,150 | $6,400 | $3,750 | **36.95%** |
| **Total** | **$86,390** | **$62,400** | **$23,990** | **27.8%** |

And just as importantly, the proposed commission is a simple **percentage of sales** — a figure we already track precisely — not a percentage of margin, so it doesn't require any margin calculation to run. It stays comfortably affordable whether the real margin is 18%, 22%, or 27% (see [economics → sensitivity](economics.md#sensitivity); the company keeps ~80%+ of gross in every case). Paying on sales is the simplest way to handle any margin uncertainty, and the exercise is also a good prompt to keep tightening margin reporting.

---

## 2. "Isn't $75k already solid pay?"

It genuinely is — a $75k base is good compensation, and nobody's suggesting otherwise. The narrower question this brief raises is whether it reflects the unusually high gross our closers generate, and what it would take to keep them:

- Each closer brings in roughly **$4.8M in sales and ~$1.3M in gross profit** a year. With the 0.6% commission, current total pay (~$103,800) works out to about **8% of that gross** — where comparable dealers structure sales pay closer to **20–30%**.
- The most useful benchmark isn't "is the salary good in the abstract?" — it's **what a competitor would pay to hire this person away.** A rival dealer could offer meaningfully more and still profit on a ~$1.3M-gross producer. Staying competitive for the people who move our inventory is what this is really about.

This isn't about any one person's pay being unfair — it's about making sure our structure keeps pace with the volume our team is now doing. → [market comparison](market-comparison.md)

---

## 3. "Leads are inbound, and we already spend a lot on lead flow — does that change the picture?"

It's a fair point, and it's already built into the proposal in two ways:

1. **The rate is set for a closing role, not a hunting one.** Because leads are company-generated, the proposed **2% of sales** is a closing-appropriate rate, well below a hunter's commission. That's exactly why the number lands near $171k, not the ~$325k a full 25%-of-gross standard would imply.
2. **Paying the closer well protects the lead investment.** Lead flow is a real, significant spend — and the closer is the point where each lead either becomes a high-ticket sale or doesn't. A strong closer converts more of those paid leads, holds margin, and attaches more of the high-margin options. So retention of a proven closer and return-on-lead-spend point in the *same* direction: keeping good closers is how we protect the money we're already putting into leads.

Fully loaded, even when lead cost is stacked on top of the proposed comp, total selling cost stays within the industry's normal envelope — and the comp portion specifically is the part that's currently light. → [cost-of-sale model](economics.md#cost-of-sale)

---

## 4. "Can we afford it?"

It's **100% variable and self-funding.** Commission is only paid on a sale that's already been booked — the gross is in the building before the commission is paid. On sales we don't make, the plan costs **$0.** There's no fixed-cost increase and no exposure in a slow month — it's pay-for-performance, funded by the performance. It may even grow the pie: rewarding option attach (36.95% margin) tends to lift gross per deal.

---

## 5. "Won't every rep want this, and does it set a precedent?"

Yes — and that's a feature. A transparent, competitive closing plan is a strong retention and recruiting signal for exactly the people who move ~$5M of product a year each. The precedent it sets — *Pine Hill pays its producers in line with what they produce* — is a competitive advantage in hiring.
