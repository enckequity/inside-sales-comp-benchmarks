# What This Role Pays Elsewhere

A salary site will show a "$67k median" for trailer salespeople. It's a fair figure to raise — this page explains why it describes a different kind of role than ours, and what the right comparables actually are.

---

## Context for the "$67k median" figure

The U.S. national median for *Sales Reps, Wholesale & Manufacturing* (the broad occupation code a trailer salesperson nominally falls under) is **~$66,780** total cash ([BLS OEWS, May 2024](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes414012.htm)). It's reasonable to ask: *"we pay $75k — aren't we already above market?"*

Here's why it describes a different role than ours:

| | "Median" rep (BLS) | A Pine Hill closer |
|---|---|---|
| Typical unit gross | ~$2,000–$5,000 | **~$22,000** |
| Volume | low, often a few units/mo | **10+/month** |
| Gross produced / year | ~$200k–$500k | **~$2.6M** |
| The job | take a small order at an average dealer | close $80k+ built-to-order units on paid inbound leads |

The BLS median describes a person who generates roughly **a tenth to a twentieth** of the gross our closers do — it's an average of mostly small, low-ticket dealers. It's a different job at a different scale, and dealer pay tracks the gross produced. Our closers produce 40–50× the gross of that benchmark rep.

---

## The right comparables

Salespeople who move **high-ticket, high-gross units at volume** are paid as a share of the large gross they generate, not against an "average salary":

- **The dealer commission standard is 20–30% of front-end gross, 25% most common** — confirmed across auto, RV, powersports, and farm/heavy equipment (full [sources, Section A](../sources.md)). The closest parallel is *Farm Equipment* magazine quoting named equipment-dealer principals at **"25% of gross on new, 30% on used."** On ~$2.6M of gross that standard rate would imply ~$500k+ — which is exactly why the proposal sits well below it.
- **RV salesperson (role-level):** average **$96,713**, 90th percentile **$114,815** ([Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/hiring/rv-salesperson-salary)); broader "RV Sales" aggregates reach ~$177k–$303k but likely include managers (directional only). These are lower-gross units than ours — the higher the unit gross, the higher the producer pay.
- **Equipment dealers (closest analog):** sales salaries are a growing share of store cost ([WEDA/NAEDA Cost of Doing Business](https://www.equipmentdealermagazine.com/weda-2019-cost-of-doing-business-study-what-we-learned/)); NADA's gold-standard [Workforce Study](https://www.nada.org/nada/research-data/nada-dealership-workforce-study-2025) is the methodological benchmark for dealer pay.
- **Authoritative trailer-specific data is gated:** NTDA's Dealer Compensation Survey and the MRAA/RVDA comp studies (see [sources, Section C](../sources.md)) — worth obtaining via membership for exact percentile figures.

The proposal's **$195k–$300k** sits below the high-line comparables and far below the literal 25%-of-gross standard. It's a conservative, defensible number.

---

## Why not pay on revenue

A note for anyone who looked up "2–10% commission" online: that figure is almost always **% of revenue (sale price)** — a different yardstick from **% of gross profit**, and the two are ~5× apart.

Equipment dealers pay on **gross profit, not revenue**, and for good reason ([Trucks Parts & Service](https://www.truckpartsandservice.com/economic-trends/indicators/article/14989063/how-do-your-sales-compensation-plans-compare): 75% of dealers pay on gross margin; [Farm Equipment](https://www.farm-equipment.com/blogs/6-opinions-columns/post/11512-why-sales-compensation-based-on-gross-margin)):

- Paying on **revenue** rewards a rep for closing a discounted deal just as much as a full-margin one — it incentivizes giving away price to make the sale.
- Paying on **gross** (or a flat per-unit amount, as proposed) aligns the rep with the dealership: protect the margin, attach the high-margin options, don't discount to close.

That's why the Pine Hill plan is **per-unit / per-gross, never a % of sale price.**

---

## Bottom line

The "$67k median" is real — but for a different, much lower-gross job. Measured against what our closers actually do (>$2.5M of gross a year on paid inbound leads), the proposed **$195k–$300k** is in line with, and toward the conservative end of, the relevant high-ticket comparables.
