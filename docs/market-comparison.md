# What This Role Pays Elsewhere

Leadership may pull up a salary site and see "$67k." This page explains why that number is the **wrong tier** for a Pine Hill closer, and what the *right* comparables actually are.

---

## The trap: the "median trailer salesperson" number

The U.S. national median for *Sales Reps, Wholesale & Manufacturing* (the broad occupation code a trailer salesperson nominally falls under) is **~$66,780** total cash ([BLS OEWS, May 2024](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes414012.htm)). Someone will wave this and say *"we pay $75k — we're already above market."*

Here's why that comparison is meaningless for us:

| | "Median" rep (BLS) | A Pine Hill closer |
|---|---|---|
| Typical unit gross | ~$2,000–$5,000 | **~$22,000** |
| Volume | low, often a few units/mo | **10+/month** |
| Gross produced / year | ~$200k–$500k | **~$2.6M** |
| The job | take a small order at an average dealer | close $80k+ built-to-order units on paid inbound leads |

The BLS median describes a person who generates **a tenth to a twentieth** of the gross our closers do. It's an average of mostly small, low-ticket dealers. Comparing it to a $2.6M-gross producer is like benchmarking a commercial-truck driver's pay against a forklift operator's because both "operate vehicles." **Same words, different job, different economics.** Pay tracks the gross produced — and ours produce 40–50× the benchmark rep's.

## The right comparables

For salespeople who move **high-ticket, high-gross units at volume** — high-line / luxury auto, Class-A motorhomes and large RVs, heavy and farm equipment, marine — top producers routinely earn **$150k–$400k+**, because their comp is set as a share of the large gross they generate, not against an "average salary":

- **Dealer-industry rule of thumb:** a salesperson's total comp runs **20–35% of the gross profit they personally produce** ([Proactive Training Solutions](https://proactivetrainingsolutions.com/dealership-pay-plan-design-compensation/)). On ~$2.6M of gross, the *literal* application is $500k–$900k — unrealistically high in practice, which is exactly why the proposal lands far below it, but it shows the direction.
- **Auto (high-volume proxy):** experienced reps $50k–$80k, **top performers >$100k** even at lower price points ([AutoFinder](https://autofinder.com/insights/how-much-commission-does-a-car-salesman-make)). Scale the unit gross up to ours and the top tier moves well into the low-to-mid six figures.
- **RV / powersports floor:** averages of $56k–$88k ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Powersports-Sales-Salary), [Glassdoor — Camping World](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salary/Camping-World-RV-Sales-Associate-Salaries-E19691_D_KO14,32.htm)) — but those are *lower-gross* units than ours; the higher the unit gross, the higher the producer pay.

The proposal's **$195k–$300k** sits *below* the high-line comparables and *far below* the literal 20–35% guideline. It's a conservative, defensible number — not a stretch.

## Why not pay on revenue

A note for anyone who looked up "2–10% commission" online: that figure is almost always **% of revenue (sale price)** — a different yardstick from **% of gross profit**, and the two are ~5× apart.

Equipment dealers pay on **gross profit, not revenue**, and for good reason ([Trucks Parts & Service](https://www.truckpartsandservice.com/economic-trends/indicators/article/14989063/how-do-your-sales-compensation-plans-compare): 75% of dealers pay on gross margin; [Farm Equipment](https://www.farm-equipment.com/blogs/6-opinions-columns/post/11512-why-sales-compensation-based-on-gross-margin)):

- Paying on **revenue** rewards a rep for closing a discounted deal just as much as a full-margin one — it incentivizes giving away price to make the sale.
- Paying on **gross** (or a flat per-unit amount, as proposed) aligns the rep with the dealership: protect the margin, attach the high-margin options, don't discount to close.

That's why the Pine Hill plan is **per-unit / per-gross, never a % of sale price.**

---

## Bottom line

The "$67k median" is real — for a different, much lower-gross job. Benchmarked against the work our closers actually do (>$2.5M of gross a year on paid inbound leads), the proposed $195k–$300k is **below** every relevant high-ticket comparable, and the current ~$75k is a severe outlier on the low side.
