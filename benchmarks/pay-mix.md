# Pay Mix (Base vs. Variable)

"Pay mix" is the split of target total cash (OTE) between guaranteed **base** and at-risk **variable** (commission). It controls how aggressive the plan feels and how much risk sits with the rep.

---

## What the data shows

| Role type | Typical base : variable | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Closing AE / full-cycle inside sales** | **50/50 to 60/40** (Bridge Group's measured SaaS-AE median is 53/47) | [Bridge Group 2024](https://blog.bridgegroupinc.com/2024-ae-metrics-compensation-benchmark); [Xactly](https://www.xactlycorp.com/blog/compensation/pay-mix-sales-compensation) |
| **SDR / non-closer** | 70/30 to 75/25 (lighter variable) | [RepVue](https://www.repvue.com/salaries/sales-development-representative) |
| **Equipment-dealer floor sales** | More aggressive — **low base/draw + commission on gross**; effectively 40/60–60/40 depending on draw | [Powersports Business](https://powersportsbusiness.com/archives/2004/04/19/developing-a-compensation-plan/), [Farm Equipment](https://www.farm-equipment.com/blogs/6-opinions-columns/post/11512-why-sales-compensation-based-on-gross-margin) |

Variable as a share of OTE follows directly: a **60/40** plan means **variable = 40% of OTE**; a 50/50 plan means half.

---

## Why a trailer dealer should be *less* aggressive than SaaS

SaaS runs 50/50 because software margins (70–90%) and giant accelerators can fund it. A physical-inventory dealer earning ~15–20% gross **can't** fund a 50/50 plan with rich accelerators without giving away the margin. So skew a few points toward base:

> **Recommended: ~60 / 40 base : variable at target OTE.**

On a ~$67k national target that's ~$40k base + ~$27k variable; on the ~$63k Lancaster target it's ~$38k base + ~$25k variable. This keeps the rep motivated by the gross-margin commission while not betting their rent on a slow trailer month.

## Quota-to-OTE ("pay-to-quota") sanity check

A common cross-industry rule: a rep should sell **4–6× their total earnings** (SaaS AE median is 4.2×; broader rule 4–6×). [Bridge Group 2024](https://blog.bridgegroupinc.com/2024-ae-metrics-compensation-benchmark); [QuotaPath](https://www.quotapath.com/calculating-ote/)

Adapt it to **gross, not revenue**: at ~22% commission on post-pack gross, a rep earning ~$27k of variable must generate ~$120k of commissionable gross — i.e., their target *gross production* should be several multiples of their pay. Use this only as a sanity check; the real constraint is Pine Hill's per-unit margin and achievable monthly unit volume, not a SaaS-derived multiple.

## Draw against commission (for ramp & seasonality)

Dealer sales rarely runs as pure salary. A **recoverable draw** — a guaranteed minimum per pay period, reconciled against earned commission — is the standard tool for:

- **New-hire ramp (first 30–90 days)**, before the pipeline produces; and
- **Seasonal slow months**, as a retention floor.

Mechanics: if commissions exceed the draw, the rep keeps the difference; if they fall short, the unearned draw is carried forward (recoverable) or forgiven (non-recoverable) per your written policy. Dealers commonly pay the draw twice monthly with a monthly commission settle-up. [Powersports Business](https://powersportsbusiness.com/archives/2004/04/19/developing-a-compensation-plan/), [Netchex](https://netchex.com/blog/how-commission-based-pay-works-for-auto-sales-staff/)

Decide recoverable vs. non-recoverable up front and put it in writing — this is the most-disputed clause in dealer pay plans.
